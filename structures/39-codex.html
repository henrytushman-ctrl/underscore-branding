What this is

Most communities are built like stages — a few people perform, the rest watch. Underscore is built like a line: flat, long, and load-bearing. Nobody stands above anyone. Everybody holds something up.

We are 1,200 builders, operators, and researchers in 40 cities who made a plain agreement: give first, ask later. It is not a slogan. It is a working method, and it has held for five years.

1
chapter onewhat this is

The economics are simple. An hour of your experience costs you almost nothing — you already paid for it the hard way. To the person stuck where you once were, that hour is worth a month.

So we trade hours the way other people trade favors, except no one keeps score. Last year the Core gave nine thousand of them. The number we watch more closely: asks that went unanswered. It was zero.

This book is short because the idea is short. The rest of it is people.

The Detroit tooling rescue

In the spring of 2024, Ana Reyes had a product and no way to make it. Her injection-molding quotes were triple her budget, and the one affordable shop had gone quiet.

She posted nine words to the Core: "Does anyone actually understand tooling? I'm out of depth."

5
chapter twothe rescue

Tom Aiello answered in forty minutes with a redesign that cut the tool count from four to two. Jonas Weber knew a shop in Stuttgart that owed him nothing but said yes anyway. Total elapsed time from ask to purchase order: eleven days.

Nobody invoiced anybody. That is the entire trick.
