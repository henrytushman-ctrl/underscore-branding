The Lead

A community that gives first.

For five years, a quiet network of builders, operators, and experts has run on one rule: offer your help before anyone asks for it. It appears to be working.

By the Editors · Oakland, Berlin & Seoul · 6 min read

The underscore is the plainest mark on the keyboard. It does not decorate. It does not interrupt. It sits beneath the line and joins words that would otherwise drift apart. That is the whole idea, and it is the whole brand: a line that connects.

Underscore began in 2021 with eleven people and a shared calendar. There was no manifesto, only a habit — when a member hit a wall, someone who had already climbed it picked up the phone. The habit became a rule. The rule became a network. Today that network is called the Core, and it numbers more than 1,200 members across 40 cities, who have given over 9,000 hours of their time to one another, unbilled and unprompted.

What members trade is not access but attention. A founder in Oakland walks an engineer in Boston through her first hardware spec. A manufacturer in Detroit opens his shop floor to a designer flying in from Mexico City. A researcher in Beirut reads a stranger's draft on a Sunday because the work deserved a careful reader.

There are no tiers, no badges, no leaderboard. The accounting is deliberately loose. "The Core gives first and asks later," as members put it — and most never get around to the asking. The return arrives anyway, the way it always does in places built on trust: slowly, then all at once.
